# Curriculum mapping and teacher evidence

Turn a selected topic into a verified teaching brief before writing animation code. This workstream addresses the meeting's concern that the team had not converted study of strong teaching into usable scripts.

## Scope and formal task

Input: board, class, school edition, language, topic, learner prerequisites, and candidate explanations. Output: a concept brief and evidence cards linking useful teaching moves to the proposed lesson.

Formally, for topic T and target learners A, select a sequence of teaching moves M that covers the required objectives O while respecting prerequisite set P and lesson duration D. Correctness and curriculum fit are hard constraints; clarity, usefulness of examples, and visual potential guide selection among valid alternatives.

The first demonstration covers one concept. Exhaustive worldwide teacher ranking and full-book digitization are outside this workstream's initial scope.

## Literature and resource implications

The [IES guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1) supports connecting concrete and abstract representations. This makes the transition from example to general rule a useful observation target. The [3Blue1Brown catalogue](https://www.3blue1brown.com/) is a candidate source of visual teaching ideas; its material still needs grade and prerequisite filtering. [PhET](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/research) offers a separate simulation-design reference. Neither resource establishes which explanation is best for a particular class.

Use the [NCERT portal](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php) to locate the school's exact edition when accessible. Its chapter contents were not verified in this review. Do not assume a chapter number from an older edition is current.

## Proposed approach

1. Ask the school's teacher to specify one measurable outcome, such as explaining why coefficients add while an unchanged variable part remains.
2. Identify prerequisite knowledge and write two brief diagnostic prompts.
3. Compare three candidate explanations: one curriculum-aligned source, one visual explanation, and one classroom teacher's approach. Three is a proposed practical starting point.
4. Record exact lesson URLs and timestamps after viewing them. Note the representation, reasoning steps, example, likely misconception, and any prerequisite gap.
5. Score each explanation from 0 to 4 for conceptual accuracy, prerequisite fit, explanatory clarity, misconception handling, and visual usefulness. Reject conceptual errors rather than averaging them away.
6. Synthesize an original explanation. Preserve source attribution and permissions for any reused asset; a public video is not automatically available for copying.
7. Have a second subject reviewer check the brief before script writing.

## Deliverables and acceptance

Deliver a one-page brief, three evidence cards, a source list, and a rejected-approach note where a tempting analogy would mislead. Acceptance requires a confirmed curriculum reference, correct prerequisite assumptions, and a reason for every selected teaching move. Do not fabricate watch timestamps or teacher endorsements.

## Ownership dependencies and evaluation

Suggested owner: a named content lead, with Sparsh as a contributor where appropriate to the meeting assignment. Dependencies: topic allocation and teacher access. The first brief is due before storyboard freeze.

Evaluate whether a different author can produce the intended script using the brief. Track missing prerequisites, unsupported claims, and reviewer corrections. A longer source list is not evidence of a better brief.

Main risk: confusing popularity with explanatory quality. Resolve it through topic-level review and short learner explanations of what they understood.
