# Scripts and instructional design

Produce an original lesson that teaches a concept through a deliberate sequence of explanation, representation, examples, and practice. The script is the contract between subject authors and video implementers.

## Scope and formal task

Given approved objectives O, prerequisites P, notation N, and evidence cards E, produce scenes S and narration A such that each objective is explained and assessed, every symbol has a stable meaning, and total video duration is at most 900 seconds. Target 300–600 seconds for the first complete lesson, as suggested in the meeting.

The first version uses a common core explanation. Supported and advanced routes are later adaptations; they are not permanent labels assigned to children.

## Literature implications

The [IES instructional guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1) motivates coordinated verbal and graphical explanations and worked-example practice. [LLM2Manim](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05266v1) provides a relevant implementation example with planned notation and synchronized narration. Its limited undergraduate study does not determine the right pace for this class.

## Proposed approach

Write the outcome first, then construct the final transfer problem. Work backward to identify the representations and reasoning steps the student needs. Open with a question whose answer requires the target concept. Add historical or real-life context only when it helps answer that question, and verify any historical attribution.

For every scene, specify what the learner should notice, exact spoken words, objects shown, transformations, pauses, and the condition that makes the representation valid. Introduce one reasoning change at a time. Connect any concrete analogy explicitly to the mathematical representation and explain where the analogy stops.

Use two worked problems with different demands: one establishes the procedure; the second exposes a misconception or applies it in a new form. Place the three student questions after the teaching section, with replay available. Keep worked answers and independent-task answers separate in the interface.

Write a notation ledger covering symbols, units, meanings, spoken forms, and first occurrence. In Hindi or Hinglish narration, review pronunciation with a fluent subject teacher. Rehearse the complete audio before claiming the planned duration is achieved.

## Later variants

| Route | Proposed change | Preserved requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supported, transcript Level 3 | Prerequisite refresh, smaller steps, additional concrete examples | Explain why the method works |
| Standard, transcript Level 2 | Core pace and representative problems | Accurate definitions and reasoning |
| Advanced, transcript Level 1 | Generalization, counterexamples, deeper connections | Clear relationship to the same objective |

## Deliverables and acceptance

Deliver narration, storyboard, notation ledger, misconception list, question map, and review comments. Acceptance requires correct concepts, an explicit concrete-to-symbol connection, no essential unstated assumption, and a feasible timing rehearsal. Reviewer sign-off should reference the script version.

Suggested owner: subject author; independent teacher reviewer. The engineer receives the approved package. A change in definition or example reopens content review.

Evaluate scripts first as narrated static storyboards. If learners cannot follow that version, animation should not be used to conceal the explanation problem. Main risks are decorative context, overloaded scenes, and polished language that skips reasoning.
