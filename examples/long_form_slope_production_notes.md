# Production notes for the slope and intercept lesson

The [narration draft](long_form_slope_script_draft.md) contains ten sections, prediction pauses, two worked problems, and three independent problems. It comes from the recorded OpenRouter pilot and has received a limited assistant edit, not subject-teacher approval. Its original generated form is preserved in the experiment folder.

## Timing and content review

The original has approximately 2,400 spoken words under the pilot's counting heuristic, excluding visual cues, headings, production notes and the appended answer table. Equations and lists make word counting approximate. Its marked pauses total 150 seconds, although the generation prompt asked for about90 seconds. With150 seconds of pauses,2,400 words require about137 words per minute to fit20 minutes. That is a planning calculation; actual narration and learner pacing may require a different duration.

Do not trim essential reasoning merely to satisfy20 minutes. Record a representative difficult section, measure speech and thinking time, then revise section budgets. A complete audio render and listening review are still needed.

The assistant edit corrected an overbroad opening claim about all straight lines, replaced language implying a mathematical line begins at its intercept, strengthened the explanation of vertical translation, qualified steepness comparisons by axis scale, and repaired an answer-table delimiter. Remaining review should examine analogy usefulness, the transition from positive to negative slopes, the practice workload, and suitability for the intended school syllabus.

## Whole lesson visual progression

| Section | Persistent state and new action | Contract to verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Motivation | Establish one coordinate board and consistent m/b colors | Explain the nonvertical-line scope; no misleading real-world linearity claim |
| 2 Intercept | Mark(0,3), substitute x=0 | Marker, label and equation use the same b |
| 3 Slope | Build y=2x+3 and a rise/run construction | Pick explicit points; signed ratio correct; equal axis units |
| 4 Prediction | Preserve graph, pause, reveal(1,5)and(2,7) | Points remain within the view; answer appears after thinking time |
| 5 Change b | Translate through b=3,−1,5 while m=2 | All labels and constructions update; old examples intentionally retained or retired |
| 6 Change m | Pivot at(0,3)through m=2,1/2,4 | Pivot invariant; clip lines to the visible range |
| 7 Edge cases | Negative, horizontal, and vertical examples | Explain that vertical lines are outside finite-m form; avoid overfilled comparison board |
| 8 Worked examples | Two-point equation, then a phone-plan model | Reframe axes explicitly; identify changed units and graph scale |
| 9 Independent work | Show problems, pause, reveal solutions progressively | Problems and answers remain distinct; preserve relevant earlier notation |
| 10 Consolidation | Return to a clean y=2x+1 construction | Restore a declared state; connect position and rate without reintroducing the entire lesson |

The phone-plan problem reaches y=23 and uses different units from the initial abstract graph. It needs an explicit reframing and axis-label change. Silently reusing a small earlier viewport would crop the important points. Equal visual units are useful for the abstract slope demonstrations; in real-world plots with different units, explain that apparent angle also depends on chosen axis scales.
