## Synthesis and originality

The lesson follows one question: how can we find a diagonal without measuring it? A right-angle route becomes a triangle, then a comparison of squares, then an area proof, then a reliable problem-solving tool. Technology contributes precise construction, conserved geometry, progressive highlighting, timed prediction pauses and reversible transformations. No source footage, diagrams, teacher voice or copied narration is incorporated. Mathematical ideas are synthesized into an original script. These sources are references, not a licensed training dataset.

## Acceptance criteria

- A student can explain what each square represents and why c is opposite the right angle.
- The proof justifies both the equal central sides and its right angles, not merely its appearance.
- Two worked problems distinguish finding a hypotenuse from finding a leg.
- Three independent questions include thinking time and explained answers.
- The learner sees why the theorem requires a right angle and why an area answer must be converted back to length.
- The final runtime is measured from audio and rendered media; a word-count estimate is not claimed as a finished 20-minute video.
